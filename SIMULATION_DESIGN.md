# Simulation design
Planned, not implemented in Phase 1. Pure TypeScript engine takes seed, versioned ratings, venue, rules and tactics; emits replayable delivery events. Use seeded PRNG only, never LLM outcomes. Combine competition priors with sample-size-aware player estimates; shrink sparse matchups toward bowling-style and handedness priors. Recency and competition weights are versioned.

First sample legal/illegal delivery, then wicket and run outcomes from normalized probabilities. Track extras, strike changes, legal-ball counts, bowler limits, dismissals and chase completion. Powerplay fielding, pitch and tactics modify probabilities with bounded effects. Live and automatic modes invoke identical transitions; live decisions pause between events. Super Overs and Impact Player rules are explicit rule modules. Replay, invariants, distribution calibration and property tests precede release. Stronger teams have an advantage without deterministic victories.

Solo auction bots compare their best projected XI before and after each signing. They assign distinct players to keeper, batting, all-rounder and bowling jobs under the four-overseas limit. New-ball pace, death bowling, spin and batting phases use the available phase estimates. A keeper-batter or all-rounder can fill the flexible batting place. Bids also reflect bench cover and remaining money per vacancy. OVR does not enter the bid formula. The XI template is a squad-building preference, not a new selection rule; prices are game estimates.

At the start of each over in new matches, bot captains compare three spin or four pace plans for the scheduled bowler. Each gets six independent trials of up to three deliveries from the current match position, including execution misses, extras, wickets and chase outcomes. Candidates share trial seeds for a fair comparison; the live match random stream stays untouched. Captains usually take the best sampled plan and sometimes the runner-up. Pre-update saves retain their original captain for replay.

The captain’s over playbook schedules one six-legal-ball preset per bowler for a selected over and innings. The marker for a wicket attempt is descriptive: it does not add a wicket bonus. Execution, the batter, pitch, field and existing pressure mechanics resolve each delivery. A wide or no-ball uses another attempt in the same slot. Once the over ends the normal bowling instructions apply.

New matches blend 70% of the delivery model with 30% of the observed outcome distribution for IPL overs 1–6, 7–15 or 16–20. This is a design weight, not a fitted coefficient. Source: Cricsheet IPL regulation innings dated 2022–2025 (ODC Attribution 1.0). Regenerate the counts with packages/cricket-data/scripts/over-context.py and the IPL JSON ZIP. Phase totals classify each delivery once; named-bowler notes use legal balls. The archive does not establish line, length, variation or a causal sequence. Existing saves retain the previous probability model.
